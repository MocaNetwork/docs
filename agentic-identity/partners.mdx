AIR Agent connects organizations that bring users and agent traffic with merchants and services that want to serve those users. The starting point is a concrete interaction where verified identity changes the experience: a member price, an eligibility check, or access to a benefit.

The roles below describe the product model. Begin pilots with account binding and credential verification through the [Agent APIs](/agentic-identity/partner-agent-api), and confirm delegation and proof-handoff support using the [availability guidance](/agentic-identity/how-it-works).

## Agent platforms and assistant builders

You provide the agent experience and help users complete tasks. AIR lets your agent carry approved identity evidence and demonstrate its authority when interacting with another application.

A first integration could connect a user's AIR Account and let the agent obtain member pricing from a participating merchant. Your product remains responsible for understanding the user's request, presenting choices, and obtaining any additional approvals the task requires.

## Publishers and applications with agent traffic

You may operate a destination that agents visit, a discovery experience that users rely on, or a marketplace connecting demand with merchants.

AIR can provide identity context for an agent interaction where the user authorizes it. This can support eligible access, customer recognition, or a handoff to a merchant that accepts the same evidence.

For a first integration, identify where agent traffic enters your product and which verified claim would change what you serve. A publisher does not need to build its own agent to participate.

## Merchants and merchant networks

You supply the products, services, and benefits. AIR helps you evaluate the evidence behind an agent request so you can apply your eligibility and offer rules.

Begin with a benefit you can define precisely, such as a member rate or first-purchase offer. Identify the credential issuers you accept, what the agent must be authorized to do, and how your checkout handles the resulting order.

The longer-term [offers model](/agentic-identity/offers-for-agents) positions merchants as the buyers of customer acquisition. Campaign pricing, attribution, and commercial terms need agreement for each program.

## Identity and loyalty providers

You already know facts about your users, such as membership or program eligibility. You can issue credentials for those facts and let users present them to accepting applications, directly or through their agents.

Your role is to define the claim, maintain its validity, and make its meaning clear to verifiers. AIR's [credential services](/airkit/usage/credential/credentials-flow) provide the underlying issuance and verification model.

## Scope the first integration

Use one journey to establish whether the integration improves the user's experience and the receiving application's decision.

| Define | Example |
| --- | --- |
| User intent | Find and purchase a product with an eligible member benefit. |
| Entry point | A personal assistant, publisher, or marketplace. |
| Accepted evidence | Membership from a named issuer. |
| User authority | Permission to present the claim to the merchant. |
| Merchant response | An eligible price with clear terms and expiry. |
| Checkout boundary | The approvals and payment method needed to complete the order. |
| Measures | Successful verification, benefit use, completed purchases, and user drop-off. |

Agree success measures and failure behavior before expanding the flow. For example, a user without a qualifying credential might receive a standard price or an issuer link, according to the merchant's rules.
