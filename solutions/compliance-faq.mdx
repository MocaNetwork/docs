## Data Storage & PII

**Does AIR Kit store our customers' personal data?**

Raw source documents remain with the issuer. Encrypted credential payloads may be stored in DStorage, while users control decryption and disclosure. The issuer signs the credential and encrypts it to the holder's public key before storage. AIR stores and routes opaque ciphertext and metadata, not plaintext issuer data.

**What does a credential actually contain?**

The credential contains the attributes defined by its schema. Prefer the minimum claims needed for the use case, such as `isOver18: true`, `kycLevel: "enhanced"`, or `tier: "Gold"`. These claims can still be personal data. The signed credential payload is encrypted before storage; it is not replaced by a hash or a boolean proof.

**Can AIR Kit or Moca Network read our customers' credential data?**

Issuers know the source data and the claims they issue. AIR receives encrypted credential payloads and metadata without the holder's decryption key. Users control decryption and disclosure. Verifiers receive the claims requested by their Verification Program and approved by the user; a ZK proof can establish a condition without disclosing the underlying value. Optional CAK access has a separate consent flow for encrypted source data.

**Where is data stored geographically?**

Source documents remain in issuer-controlled systems. Encrypted credential payloads may be stored with DStorage providers, alongside storage metadata. On-chain records and off-chain verification sessions are separate from those payloads. Confirm provider locations, metadata processing, and any regional commitments with the AIR team when assessing data residency. Encryption alone does not make identifiable data anonymous; see the [European Commission's GDPR guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/application-gdpr_en).

## GDPR

**Is this compliant with GDPR Article 5(1)(c) — data minimisation?**

AIR Kit supports data minimisation through schema design, encryption, and program-controlled disclosure. Choose only the claims needed for your purpose and request only the fields your verifier needs. Compliance depends on your implementation and processing purposes; using derived claims or encryption does not establish it automatically.

**How do we handle Article 17 data subject deletion requests?**

Revocation prevents a credential from passing subsequent checks that recognise its revoked status. It does not delete encrypted payloads, metadata, source records, or data already disclosed to verifiers. Handle erasure and retention separately across issuer systems, storage providers, and verifiers, including any applicable retention obligations. On-chain records cannot be removed by revoking a credential.

**Does issuing credentials constitute international data transfer under GDPR Chapter V?**

Assess the actual data flow: encrypted credential payloads and metadata may be stored outside the issuer's systems, and verifiers may receive user-approved claims or CAK-protected data. Review provider locations, recipients, and applicable transfer safeguards with your data protection team. Do not assume that encryption or ZK proofs remove transfer obligations.

**Who is the data controller?**

Issuers control their source data and signing keys. Verifiers determine how they use disclosed claims. Establish controller and processor roles for each processing activity in your agreements, including AIR's handling of ciphertext, metadata, and verification sessions. The storage architecture alone does not determine every party's legal role.

**Does consent need to be obtained to issue credentials?**

Determine and document the lawful basis for issuance with your data protection team. User controls for decryption and disclosure, including explicit consent for CAK access, are separate from that legal assessment. Direct issuance does not remove your notice or lawful-basis requirements.

## CCPA / CPRA

**Does issuing AIR Kit credentials constitute "selling" personal information under CCPA?**

Assess your actual processing, disclosures, purposes, and contracts. Credential issuance and verification can involve encrypted personal information, metadata, and user-approved claims; it is not a blanket exemption from sale or sharing rules. See the [California Attorney General's CCPA guidance](https://www.oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa).

**How do we handle opt-out requests?**

Apply the opt-out to the relevant processing and sharing activities. Revocation can stop future use of a credential, but it does not retract data already disclosed or replace your opt-out and deletion procedures.

## Security & Breach Liability

**What is the breach liability for verifiers accepting AIR Kit credentials?**

A proof-only program can reduce the data a verifier receives. Successful verification returns a Verifiable Presentation that can contain user-approved claims and optional proof material. Verifiers must protect any disclosed claims, identifiers, session records, and CAK-decrypted data they retain. Exposure depends on the program and the verifier's data handling.

**What is the breach liability for issuers?**

Issuers hold source data and signing keys and prepare the credential before encrypting it to the holder. AIR's storage and routing layer handles ciphertext and metadata. Assess exposure across source systems, issuer keys, encrypted storage, metadata, and any disclosed copies; encryption does not eliminate every breach risk or liability.

**Is there an audit trail for credential issuance and verification?**

AIR Kit uses verification sessions for usage and reporting. Whether a verification proof is submitted on-chain depends on the Verification Program. Do not assume every issuance or verification event appears in a single on-chain audit log; confirm the records available for your integration.

## Credentials & Revocation

**How quickly does credential revocation take effect?**

Revocation is checked during verification. The effect depends on the issuer's revocation mechanism and when the updated status is available to the verifier. Do not assume a universal one-block or instant propagation guarantee.

**What happens to credentials if a user closes their account?**

Include credential revocation in your account-closure process and handle source records, encrypted payloads, metadata, and previously disclosed data under your retention and deletion procedures. Account closure or revocation does not by itself erase all copies.

**Can a user hold credentials from multiple issuers in one AIR Account?**

Yes. An AIR Account can hold credentials from any number of issuers. The user controls which credentials to present at any given verifier.

**Can credentials expire?**

Yes. You set an `expirationDate` in the credential subject. After expiry, the credential returns NON_COMPLIANT. You can re-issue with a new expiry at any time.

## Compliance Access Key (CAK)

**What is the Compliance Access Key (CAK)?**

CAK is an optional encryption framework within AIR Kit designed for regulated industries where verifiers need access to raw personal data (ID images, biometric photos). It adds user-consent-driven encryption at issuance and controlled decryption at verification, ensuring data sovereignty remains with the user at all times.

**How does CAK differ from standard credential verification?**

The Verification Program determines requested claims, whether a ZK proof is required, and whether verification is off-chain or on-chain. Successful verification returns a Verifiable Presentation with the approved disclosures and any required proof. CAK adds consent-controlled access to separately encrypted source data when a verifier needs to review identity documents or other underlying records.

**Do I need to enable CAK?**

Not necessarily. CAK is optional. If your credentials only need ZK proof-based verification (attribute proofs without revealing raw data), the standard flow is sufficient. Enable CAK when raw PII access by verifiers is a regulatory requirement, when multiple verifiers need the same underlying data, or when you need an auditable consent trail for data access.

**Who controls whether CAK is active?**

Both Issuers and Verifiers make independent decisions. Issuers enable CAK on their pricing schemas and issuance programs. Verifiers choose whether to require CAK on their verification programs. The user always has the final say — they must explicitly consent before any data is decrypted.

**Can the platform or Moca Network decrypt CAK-encrypted data?**

No. The CAK private key is generated locally on the user's device and only shared with the Verifier after explicit user consent. The platform never holds the private key and cannot decrypt the data.

For full technical details, see the [CAK overview](/airkit/usage/credential/cak-overview), [Issuer guide](/airkit/usage/credential/cak-issuer-guide), and [Verifier guide](/airkit/usage/credential/cak-verifier-guide).
