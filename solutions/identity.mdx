Every platform that needs to verify a user's identity runs its own KYC process and stores a copy of the result. AIR Kit lets you **issue a KYC credential once** that every partner in the ecosystem can verify via zero-knowledge proof, without raw identity data ever crossing the network.

## The Problem

Every platform that needs to verify a user's identity runs its own KYC process, stores a copy of the result, and takes on the liability for protecting it. The user goes through the same friction — passport upload, liveness check, waiting period — at every new service they join.

The industry has accepted this as the cost of compliance. It isn't.

## What Changes With AIR Kit

- **Your customer verifies once** — KYC passed at your platform issues a portable credential. Every partner in the ecosystem accepts it without running KYC again
- **Control what verifiers receive** — Programs can request ZK proofs of eligibility, specific claims with user approval, or consent-controlled CAK access when underlying documents are required
- **Your KYC provider doesn't change** — You keep Jumio, Onfido, Persona, or whoever you use. AIR Kit converts their pass result into a portable credential. The verification itself stays with your provider
- **Limit copies of identity data** — Raw source documents remain with the issuer or its KYC provider. Encrypted credential payloads may be stored in DStorage, and users control decryption and disclosure. Verifiers remain responsible for protecting any data they receive

## How It Works

<Steps>
  <Step title="User completes KYC with your provider">
    Your existing flow. Your existing KYC provider. Nothing changes for the user or your team at this step.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your backend issues a KYC credential">
    The moment your KYC provider returns a pass result, your backend calls on-demand issuance. One call. The credential issues silently — no user action needed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Credential lands in the user's AIR Account">
    The user can access an issuer-signed credential encrypted to their public key and stored in DStorage. The issuer retains the source documents, while the user controls decryption and disclosure of the credential.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Any partner verifies via ZK proof">
    At a partner service, the user approves the requested disclosure. The partner receives a compliance result and, on success, a Verifiable Presentation containing the approved claims and any required proof.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What the Verifier Actually Sees

The following example uses a program that requests KYC status and limited claims with user approval. Other programs can request different disclosures or CAK access.

| What the Verifier Receives | What Stays Private |
|----------------------------|--------------------|
| COMPLIANT / NON_COMPLIANT | Full name, passport number |
| KYC level (basic / enhanced / full) | Date of birth |
| `isOver18: true` | Residential address |
| Credential expiry date | National ID / tax number |
| Issuer identifier | Income, net worth, financial data |

## What Compliance Will Ask

**Is this GDPR Article 5(1)(c) compliant — data minimisation?**
Use the minimum claims needed for the check, such as KYC level and `isOver18`. These derived claims can still be personal data. Credential encryption and user-controlled disclosure support minimisation, but compliance depends on your implementation.

**How do we handle data subject deletion requests?**
Revoke the credential to stop future successful checks once its revoked status is available. Handle deletion of source records, encrypted payloads, metadata, and disclosed copies separately. See the [Compliance FAQ](/solutions/compliance-faq).

**Who has access to the underlying identity documents?**
Raw source documents remain with the issuer or its KYC provider. AIR may store encrypted credential payloads in DStorage. If CAK is enabled for source-data access, a verifier can decrypt the separately encrypted data only after explicit user consent.

**What if a user disputes their KYC result?**
The dispute process stays entirely with your KYC provider, as it does today. You revoke or re-issue the AIR Kit credential once the dispute resolves.

**Does AIR Kit have access to our users' data?**
Your backend signs the credential and encrypts it to the holder before storage. AIR stores and routes ciphertext and metadata, not plaintext issuer data. The user controls decryption and disclosure; encrypted claims and metadata may still be personal data.
