---

## What the user provides

Typical inputs across programmatic and manual paths include:

| Category | Examples | Purpose |
|----------|----------|---------|
| Identity document | Passport, national ID, driver's license, residence permit | Document authenticity, MRZ/OCR, chip read where NFC applies |
| Biometrics | Selfie images or video, liveness challenge | Match user to document portrait; fraud resistance |
| Device signals | IP address, coarse geo, device metadata | Risk scoring and sanctions workflows |
| NFC chip payload | ePassport / eID chip data | Cryptographic authenticity (zkPassport path) |

Exact fields depend on the verification program configured for your partnership. **Your application does not need to persist these artifacts** to participate in the AIR credential model.

---

## What the partner receives

| Data | Partner receives? |
|------|-------------------|
| Passport / ID images | No — not for routine verification-by-ZK |
| MRZ text, chip dumps | No |
| Selfie / liveness media | No |
| AML raw hits | No — policy outputs are reflected in the **credential** your programs define |
| **AIR Credential** reference and attributes | Yes — only what the Issuance program encodes (for example `kycLevel`, `isOver18`, compliance flags) |
| **Verifier result** | Yes — **COMPLIANT** / **NON_COMPLIANT** (and related proof metadata from AIR Kit) |

Partners should treat the credential as the **integration contract**: define minimal attributes, avoid storing duplicates of identity media, and rely on revocation and re-issuance when users or regulators require updates.

---

## What Moca Chain stores

Moca Chain records **cryptographic commitments** for credentials — for example hashes, Merkle roots, and revocation state — not plaintext PII. That design is what lets verifiers trust a credential without inheriting a document vault.

---

## What zkMe Labs holds

Under **Veriff powered by zkMe**, zkMe Labs operates the verification orchestration and **encrypts sensitive verification payloads** under the zkMe **zkVault** model (hardware-backed trusted execution, threshold encryption, and supporting cryptography — summarized on [zkMe security architecture](/kyc/zkme-security-architecture)).

zkMe uses retained material only for lawful purposes you would expect from a regulated identity provider: session quality, fraud investigation, re-verification, regulatory inquiry support, and credential refresh — not for resale to advertisers.

---

## How the verification stack fits together

End-user verification sessions for **Veriff powered by zkMe** may execute on infrastructure operated under zkMe Labs' commercial and technical arrangements. Outputs are consumed by **zkMe Labs** as the **legal and contracting party** for your Moca-facing KYC bundle. Partners contract for this path with **zkMe Labs** only.

Always refer to the offering in public materials as **Veriff powered by zkMe** — use the full product name, not a shortened form.

## What the verifier sees (ZK path)

| Verifier may receive | Stays private by default |
|----------------------|---------------------------|
| COMPLIANT / NON_COMPLIANT | Full legal name on ID |
| Program-defined attributes (for example `kycLevel`) | Passport / national ID number |
| Credential identifier, expiry, issuer | Date of birth, full address |
| Proof metadata from verification | Document images, selfie video |

Exact fields depend on your **credential schema** and **verification program** configuration in the Developer Dashboard.
