AIR Identity lets you check a fact about a user, for example - their age, KYC level, or membership tier, without collecting the documents behind it.

TLDR: A business that already knows the user issues a credential. The user keeps it in their AIR Account and shares only what you ask for, after approving each request.

This page covers what you receive and what stays out of your systems. For how to build it, see [For developers](#for-developers).

## What you get back

**When you verify a credential**, you receive:

| You receive | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| A result status | Whether the credential meets your rules: `Compliant`, or a reason it doesn't, such as `Expired`, `Revoked`, or `NotFound` |
| The claims you asked for, and only those the user approved | For example `isOver18: true`, `kycLevel: "enhanced"`, or `tier: "Gold"` |
| The issuer's signature | Which business vouched for the claims, and that nobody changed them |
| A binding to the user | For holder-bound credentials, proof that the person presenting is the person it was issued to |

You choose what to ask for in a verification program in the Developer Dashboard. Ask for less, and less reaches you.

**When you issue a credential:**

- It is signed with your keys under your own `did:web` identity, so every verifier sees that it came from you.
- Users take it to other apps, which verify it without calling your API.
- You can revoke it when the facts change, and later checks fail.

For how issuers and verifiers earn from this, see [Partner Economics](/solutions/partner-economics).

## What you never store

**As a verifier**, these never reach you:

- **Source documents.** ID images, selfies, and the issuer's records stay with the issuer.
- **Claims you didn't ask for**, or that the user declined to share.
- **The user's keys.** AIR Account keys are split with MPC, so no single party, including you and AIR, holds a full key.

**As an issuer**, you don't host the credential for anyone else. It is encrypted to the user and stored in Moca Chain's decentralized storage, and you never hold the key that decrypts it.

**AIR** stores the encrypted credential and its metadata, never the plaintext. To identify an AIR Account across partners, AIR keeps a hashed email.

<Note>
The claims you do receive can still be personal data. Your own retention and deletion rules apply to them, and revoking a credential does not delete copies already shared. See the [Compliance FAQ](/solutions/compliance-faq).
</Note>
